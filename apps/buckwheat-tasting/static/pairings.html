Gastronomy  ·  The Art of the Considered Table

The Pairing Guide

A cultivar of excellence requires its accompaniments to be chosen with equivalent care. The following pairings have been developed and tested by the Buckwheat Spectator culinary panel over four years of systematic evaluation.

Formal Table
Mancan Reserve 2019 & Sevruga Caviar on Blini

The canonical pairing for the Allegheny appellation's highest expression. The iron-rich mineral depth of the Mancan finds its saline counterpoint in the Sevruga's oceanic weight, while the blini provides a textural bridge of cultured grain. Use cultured butter, not sweet; serve both at precisely 8°C. Pour the Mancan at 12°C, no cooler, and allow five minutes of open-air exposure before the first evaluation. This pairing requires no accompaniment and no conversation.

Petrovsky Reserve 2020 & Beluga Caviar

A pairing of geographic inevitability: two products of the Volga basin, separated by taxonomy but united by terroir. The Petrovsky's leather-and-fennel mid-palate resonates with the buttery brininess of the Beluga in a manner that this critic can only describe as historically justified. Serve with smetana and a single shot of Beluga Noble vodka, consumed before the buckwheat is tasted and not repeated.

Bourgeois-Lefebvre Grand Cru 2018 & Fine de Claire Oysters

The Breton cultivar and the Breton oyster were, one suspects, designed for each other by forces that preceded either producer's existence. The iodine and wet schist of the Bourgeois-Lefebvre finds its mirror in the oceanic minerality of the Claire oyster. Serve the oysters on crushed ice with a half-lemon; serve the cultivar at room temperature in a shallow white porcelain vessel. Migrate between the two without haste.

Koto Single-Origin 2021 & Scallop Sashimi

Japanese simplicity elevated to the level of philosophical proposition. The Koto's pristine mineral clarity finds its expression in scallop of the freshest provenance, barely dressed with ponzu and a sliver of yuzu zest. The umami quality of the cultivar echoes the sweetness of the scallop without overpowering it. This pairing is at its finest in silence, with one's attention directed inward.

Allegheny Reserve & 36-month Comté

The aged Comté's crystalline texture and complex tyrosine depth matches the iron-and-hazelnut character of a fine Allegheny Reserve with a reliability that makes it the standard formal cheese pairing for this appellation. The Comté must be aged a minimum of 36 months; younger versions are agreeable but do not achieve the necessary counterweight. Serve at room temperature on a slate board with no bread and no fruit.

Koban Reserve 2022 & Miso Soup with Wakame

A pairing of extraordinary subtlety. The Koban's silky texture and understated umami find their most eloquent expression alongside a white miso soup of exceptional quality. The wakame's marine character amplifies the mineral note of the cultivar; the miso's fermented depth echoes the grain character without overwhelming it. This pairing rewards a composed and unhurried state of mind.

Brunch and Morning Table
Pennquad Heritage & Buckwheat Pancake, Pure Maple Syrup

The Pennquad's honest, grain-forward character and the warmth of a properly made heritage buckwheat pancake constitute the definitive morning presentation for the Pennsylvania Dutch appellation. The maple syrup must be Grade A Dark (not Medium Amber, which is too forward); cultured cream replaces the indignity of commercial butter. A preparation of uncommon elegance in its apparent simplicity.

Horizon Reserve & Soft-Boiled Farm Egg with Soldiers

The Horizon's warm, accessible character and the clean saline simplicity of a perfectly soft-boiled egg from a heritage-breed hen constitute a pairing of domestic comfort elevated by attention. The toast soldiers should be of a sourdough bread made with Manor or Horizon flour. Do not use commercial bread.

Springfield Atlantique & Galette Complete

The Breton galette complete — ham, egg, and aged emmental folded in a properly made sarrasin crepe — is the canonical regional preparation and the natural expression of the Springfield Atlantique's warm, butter-and-salt character. The galette must be prepared with stone-ground sarrasin flour from the Morbihan appellation; commercial blends are unacceptable and will not achieve the necessary texture.

Late Night and Contemplative
Mancan Reserve & Aged Pipe Tobacco (as context, not consumption)

The critic Margaux Estienne-Beauvoir has observed that the finest expression of the Mancan Reserve's tobacco-and-slate finish is experienced in the presence of aged pipe tobacco. The tobacco is not smoked; it is, rather, present as an ambient olfactory reference that allows the cultivar's finishing character to be evaluated against an external standard. This observation has been met with both admiration and bewilderment by the critical community, which constitutes, in this journal's experience, evidence of its correctness.

Petrovsky Reserve & Dark Rye Bread, Unsalted

At the end of a serious evening, when the elaborate preparations of the formal table have been exhausted and only honesty remains, the Petrovsky Reserve and a slice of dark rye bread, without embellishment, constitutes perhaps the most authentic expression of the cultivar's character. The bread's dense, slightly bitter grain note clears the palate between evaluations; the Petrovsky fills the space made available with its characteristic weight and length.

Picnic and Informal
Horizon Reserve & Farmhouse Cheese Board

The Horizon's pastoral warmth and the assembled pleasures of a well-considered cheese board constitute the ideal outdoor pairing. Aged cheddar (2-year minimum), a firm 12-month Gouda, and a runny Epoisses, arranged on a slate with honeycomb from local hives, allow the cultivar's multiple registers to find their counterparts in sequence. A summer afternoon in the Pennsylvania hills is the recommended setting; no other is quite adequate.

Manor Select & Charcuterie

The Manor Select's almond-and-fig character and the cured meats of the Allegheny farmhouse tradition constitute a pairing of honest pleasure. Air-cured pork loin, country ham of at least 24 months, and a hard salami of Italian inspiration, served with cornichons and a grainy mustard, allow the cultivar's structure to assert itself without demanding the occasion be a formal one.

Pennquad Heritage & Aged Sharp Cheddar, Apple Butter

A pairing of Pennsylvania Dutch Country completeness. The Pennquad's fresh-grain character and the concentrated tang of a properly aged sharp cheddar from the Lancaster corridor constitute a marriage of regional logic. The apple butter, spread sparingly on a heel of sourdough, provides the sweet note that the cultivar's savouriness requires as contrast. No further elaboration is necessary or advisable.

Holiday and Occasion
Petrovsky Reserve & Foie Gras Terrine, Christmas Table

The Christmas table at a serious household should open with the Petrovsky Reserve presented alongside a foie gras terrine of the finest accessible provenance. The cultivar's black-loam density and the foie gras's adipose richness achieve, together, a weight that is appropriate to the occasion and the season. Serve the terrine at precisely 4°C; serve the buckwheat at room temperature of no more than 18°C. Use Sauternes as the ambient beverage if one must use a beverage at all.

Koto Single-Origin & New Year's Soba

The Japanese tradition of Toshikoshi soba — the long noodles eaten on New Year's Eve to symbolise the crossing of years — achieves its highest expression with Koto Single-Origin flour. Prepare the noodles by hand; use a tsuyu of exceptional dashi quality. The cultivar's pure, clear character, consumed in this context, carries a weight of cultural continuity that no other pairing available to the buckwheat connoisseur can match.

Buckwheat Cocktail: The Groat Old Fashioned

The preparation: 2oz aged rye whiskey, 1 barspoon buckwheat honey syrup (equal parts honey and water, with 2 tablespoons of Pennquad Heritage flour dissolved by gentle heat and strained), 2 dashes Angostura bitters, large ice cube, expressed orange peel. The buckwheat honey syrup introduces a mineral grain note that complements the rye without competition. Serve in a heavy crystal old fashioned glass; do not stir beyond 30 rotations.
