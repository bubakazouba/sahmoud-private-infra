Editorial Panel  ·  The Voices of Record

The Critics

The Buckwheat Spectator's critical panel comprises five individuals whose combined experience spans six continents, eleven appellations, and thirty-eight years of systematic groat evaluation.

M.E
Margaux Estienne-Beauvoir
Editor-in-Chief & Senior Critic  ·  Master of Buckwheat, Hon. PhD

Margaux Estienne-Beauvoir founded the Buckwheat Spectator in 1987 following a decade of work in the agricultural sciences at the Institut National Agronomique in Paris. Her 1985 doctoral research on the mineral chemistry of Breton buckwheat terroir established the theoretical foundations of the Estienne-Beauvoir Scoring System, which she published in its first complete form in the inaugural issue of this journal. In the thirty-eight years since, she has evaluated more cultivars, across more appellations, than any other living critic. Her tasting notes are distinguished by their severity, their precision, and an occasional lyrical quality that reveals, beneath the scientific apparatus, a critic who is also, unmistakably, a poet. She farms 47 acres of volcanic loam at Upper Ojai, California, sustainably since 2003.

"The 2019 Mancan Reserve delivers a palate of uncommon depth and intellectual rigour that this critic has not encountered in three decades of serious groat study. Compelling, even humbling. One pours slowly, and says nothing." On Domaine Mancan Reserve 2019, 98 pts, December 2019
H.S
Henrik Solberg-Ostrowski
Eastern European Affairs  ·  Master of Buckwheat

Born in Bergen, Norway, and educated at the Norwegian University of Life Sciences before completing his MB at the Institut Fagopyrum in 1997, Solberg-Ostrowski has spent the larger part of three decades in the Volga Basin appellations, developing what is widely regarded as the most comprehensive expertise in Russian buckwheat production available to any Western critic. His prose style is laconic to the point of severity; where Estienne-Beauvoir reaches for the literary, Solberg-Ostrowski offers the geological. His assessments of the Petrovsky estate are the authoritative record of that producer's considerable achievements. He divides his time between Saratov and Bergen, where he maintains a cellar of extraordinary depth.

"The Petrovsky Reserve 2020 is a cultivar of geological weight. The chernozem speaks directly, without mediation, through sixty seconds of finish that this critic found impossible to dismiss and difficult to describe. The honest response is silence; the professional one is 97 points." On Domaine de Petrovsky Reserve 2020, 97 pts, September 2021
Y.N
Yuki Nakamura-Estienne
Asia-Pacific Bureau Chief  ·  Master of Buckwheat

The daughter of a Kyoto soba master and a Hokkaido soil scientist, Nakamura-Estienne brings to her criticism a dual inheritance that no other member of this panel can claim: the practitioner's understanding of buckwheat as culinary material and the scientist's knowledge of the agronomy that produces it. She completed the MBA at the Institut Fagopyrum in 2004 and has since established the Asian bureau of the Buckwheat Spectator as its most productive regional office, covering twelve Japanese prefectures, South Korea, and an annual survey of Chinese Tartary buckwheat production that remains the only English-language assessment of those appellations in print. She is, by common agreement, the finest taster on the panel.

"The Koto Single-Origin Estate 2021 arrives in a whisper of extraordinary refinement. This critic returned to it three times while writing these notes; each return revealed something not previously perceived. It is, at 95 points, either the finest Hokkaido cultivar ever produced or the finest this critic has yet encountered. The distinction may not matter." On Koto Single-Origin Estate 2021, 95 pts, November 2021
A.V
Anastasia Volkov-Brennan
Volga Basin Correspondent  ·  Associate Buckwheat Sommelier

Volkov-Brennan's contributions to this journal are unusual in that they reach the editorial office not by electronic means but by post, on paper, in a hand that one senior colleague has described as "resembling a seismograph recording a minor earthquake." She writes from the Saratov growing region during harvest season and from her family home in County Clare, Ireland, during the winter months. Her assessments of the Volga Basin's secondary estates are without peer; her tendency to score generously relative to the senior panel is a known quantity that the editors have not seen fit to correct. She holds the ABS with the highest examination score on record.

"The Solberg-Ostrowski Stepnaya 2021 is a cultivar of intellectual rather than hedonistic appeal. It does not flatter; it insists. Those willing to meet it on its own terms will find a reward of the specifically Russian kind: severe, complete, and not easily forgotten." On Solberg-Ostrowski Stepnaya 2021, 90 pts, April 2022
F.M
Francois-Xavier Morin-Duhamel
Brittany & Northern France  ·  Buckwheat Professional

A native of Quimper whose family has farmed sarrasin in the Finistere for four generations, Morin-Duhamel brings to his criticism the particular authority of one who has not merely tasted but cultivated. He holds the BP designation from the Institut Fagopyrum Dijon, where he also teaches the Breton module of the certification curriculum. His assessments of the Bourgeois-Lefebvre estate are widely considered the definitive critical record of that producer, a view that Morin-Duhamel himself disputes on grounds of what he terms "insufficient severity." He is also, in the view of this editorial board, the finest prose stylist on the panel, a distinction he would consider irrelevant to the task of groat evaluation.

"The Bourgeois-Lefebvre Grand Cru 2018 arrives like the opening bars of a Debussy prelude: immediately, completely, and with the confident authority of an entity that knows it does not need to announce itself. The Atlantic salt spray note is the most precisely expressed coastal mineral character this critic has encountered from any appellation. Ninety-six points, and this critic would not argue with ninety-seven." On Chateau Bourgeois-Lefebvre Grand Cru 2018, 96 pts, June 2019
The Estienne-Beauvoir Scoring Methodology

All scores issued by the Buckwheat Spectator are determined by a panel process in which each evaluating critic scores independently, under blind conditions, and the final score is issued as the mean of panel scores, adjusted by the Editor-in-Chief for systematic outliers. The following score bands carry defined meanings:

97-100Legendary. A cultivar that transcends its category and establishes a new reference point for the appellation. Fewer than ten scores in this range have been issued in 38 years of publication.
94-96Outstanding. A cultivar of exceptional quality from which the serious connoisseur should secure an allocation without delay. The 94 represents excellence of the highest accessible order; the 96 represents something approaching transcendence.
90-93Excellent. A cultivar of serious quality and reliable character. The 90-point threshold represents the minimum level at which a cultivar merits the attention of a connoisseur for whom quality is the primary consideration.
86-89Very Good. A cultivar of honest quality that delivers more than its price suggests and less than the appellation's finest. Suitable for daily evaluation by the serious but not solemnly dedicated connoisseur.
80-85Good. A competent cultivar without distinction. The Spectator does not review cultivars below 80 points; the panel's time is finite and better allocated to the assessment of quality.
