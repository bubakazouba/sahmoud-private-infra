Koban Reserve Niigata

2022 Vintage  ·  Niigata Prefecture, Japan  ·  Reserve
Reviewed by Yuki Nakamura-Estienne MBA  ·  Buckwheat Spectator, April 2023

Critical Assessment

Nose:

If the Koto Estate is a haiku, the Koban Reserve is a tanka: a step longer in form, a fraction warmer in register. Green tea of the highest ceremonial grade opens the aromatic passage, followed swiftly by river stone and the pleasant austerity of young wheat before full maturation. There is an alpine water quality to this cultivar that speaks of the snowmelt fed terraces of the Uonuma basin, where the Sea of Japan's influence moderates the mountain cold into something altogether more hospitable than the Hokkaido plateau.

Palate:

Clean and precise in the fashion of all Niigata cultivars at their finest, with a silky texture that is the defining achievement of this particular valley. The mid-palate carries a subtly sweet quality — rice-syrup, not sugar — that resolves into a long, clean, mineral finish. There is a completeness to the Koban 2022 that earns this critic's unqualified admiration: it knows exactly what it is and performs that identity with uncommon grace.

Overall:

The finest Niigata cultivar currently available from the Spectator's regular review programme. The Koban Reserve 2022 is a cultivar for moments of quiet reflection and calibrated pleasure.

Provenance

Koban was developed by the Niigata Agricultural Research Institute in 1993 to take advantage of the prefecture's famous koshihikari rice-growing conditions. The cultivar has since established a reputation for exceptional textural refinement that no other Japanese buckwheat variety has surpassed. The Uonuma Reserve program selects from terraces at 200-350 metres, irrigated by Uono River snowmelt from the Echigo Mountains. Farming is entirely without synthetic inputs; the farming families are compensated at double the regional standard to maintain this commitment.

Pairings

The Koban Reserve's silky texture makes it a natural companion to preparations of similar refinement. Warm zaru soba — ideally from this cultivar's own flour — served with a dipping sauce of exceptional dashi, is the canonical presentation. Grilled river trout, barely seasoned, allows the mineral character to fully express. Oysters, ideally from the Sea of Japan, provide a saline counterpoint of pleasing precision.

Cellar Notes

Washi-wrapped tins with tamper-evident cord. Store at 8-13°C, 50-60% relative humidity. The silky texture is best preserved at lower storage temperatures; compromise on this point and the defining quality of the Koban character is the first casualty. Optimal consumption: 3-8 months post-milling.
