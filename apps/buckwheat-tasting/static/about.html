About the Buckwheat Spectator

Thirty-eight years of serious groat scholarship, from a one-page mimeograph distributed at the Dijon Cereal Sciences Conference to the international journal of record for Fagopyrum connoisseurship.

Our History

Since 1987

The Buckwheat Spectator was founded in September 1987 by Margaux Estienne-Beauvoir, then a postdoctoral researcher in cereal agronomy at the Institut National Agronomique in Paris. The inaugural issue — four pages, mimeographed, distributed to eleven colleagues at the Dijon Cereal Sciences Conference — contained a single review (the 1986 Mancan Reserve from the Allegheny Plateau, awarded 91 points) and an editorial arguing that the absence of a rigorous critical vocabulary for buckwheat evaluation was a scandal that would not resolve itself.

By 1991, the journal had grown to 32 pages and was distributed by subscription to 340 readers in 12 countries. The Estienne-Beauvoir Scoring System was formalised in Volume IV (1990), establishing the 100-point scale and the eight primary aroma categories that remain the discipline's standard evaluation framework. The following year, Margaux Estienne-Beauvoir left the Institut National Agronomique to found the California estate that bears her name and to devote herself entirely to the journal's editorial mission.

The 1990s brought rapid expansion. The establishment of the Asian bureau under Yuki Nakamura-Estienne in 1997 extended the journal's coverage to the Japanese appellations; the appointment of Henrik Solberg-Ostrowski as Eastern European correspondent in 1995 provided the authoritative voice on Russian production that the critical community had long required. By 2000, the Buckwheat Spectator was the world's only dedicated fagopyrum journal with an active contributing panel on three continents.

The first Concours International de Fagopyrum, held in Dijon in 1989, was conceived by Estienne-Beauvoir as a complement to the critical programme: where the journal provided the vocabulary of evaluation, the competition provided the forum for producers to test their work against the best available standard. Thirty-eight years later, the Concours remains the discipline's definitive competitive event. The 2026 edition, the first on North American soil, is a landmark that the Competition Committee does not pretend to approach with equanimity.

Editorial Charter

What We Stand For

The Buckwheat Spectator was founded on a single proposition: that buckwheat, Fagopyrum esculentum and its relatives, is a crop of sufficient complexity and cultural depth to support the same rigorous critical apparatus that has long been applied to wine, tea, and olive oil. This proposition has not been universally accepted, and the journal has not always been extended the respect that its editorial standards warrant. We consider this a matter of time rather than principle.

All scores issued by the Buckwheat Spectator are the product of blind evaluation conducted under controlled conditions. No critic accepts hospitality, remuneration, or any consideration from producers whose cultivars are under review. The editorial board does not accept advertising from producers; the journal's independence is maintained by subscription revenue and the Patron tier alone.

We are aware that our seriousness on this subject is, to some observers, amusing. We find this response illuminating as to the observer's assumptions about which agricultural products merit sustained critical attention, and we do not engage with it further.
